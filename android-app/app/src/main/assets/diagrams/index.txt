cell_animal.png|Animal Cell|Cells and Organelles|An animal cell has a cell membrane, jelly-like cytoplasm and a nucleus that controls the cell. Mitochondria release energy, ribosomes make proteins, and the Golgi apparatus packs and sends materials. Unlike plant cells, animal cells have no cell wall and no chloroplasts.
cell_plant.png|Plant Cell|Cells and Organelles|A plant cell has a strong cell wall outside the membrane, a large vacuole filled with cell sap, and chloroplasts for photosynthesis. The nucleus controls all activities. The wall gives the cell a fixed shape and support.
mitochondrion.png|Mitochondrion|Cells and Organelles|The mitochondrion is the powerhouse of the cell. Its inner membrane is folded into cristae, and the inner fluid is called the matrix. Cellular respiration happens here and releases energy in the form of ATP.
chloroplast.png|Chloroplast|Cells and Organelles|Chloroplasts are found only in plant cells. They contain the green pigment chlorophyll, which traps sunlight. Inside, flat sacs called thylakoids are stacked into grana, surrounded by a fluid called stroma. Photosynthesis takes place here.
bacteria.png|Bacterial Cell|Cells and Organelles|A bacterium is a prokaryotic cell: it has no proper nucleus. Its DNA lies in a region called the nucleoid, and small extra DNA rings are called plasmids. It has a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and some bacteria have a capsule and flagella for movement.
neuron.png|Neuron (Nerve Cell)|Human Body|A neuron carries messages in the nervous system. Dendrites receive signals, the cell body contains the nucleus, and the long axon carries the signal away. Many axons are covered by a Schwann cell sheath. At the end, the synapse passes the message to the next cell.
heart.png|Human Heart|Human Body|The heart has four chambers: two upper atria and two lower ventricles. The right side receives low-oxygen blood and sends it to the lungs; the left side receives oxygen-rich blood and pumps it to the whole body. Valves stop the blood from flowing backwards.
digestive.png|Digestive System|Human Body|Food travels through the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine. The liver, pancreas and salivary glands add juices that digest food. Digested food is absorbed in the small intestine, and water is absorbed in the large intestine.
kidney.png|Human Kidney|Human Body|Inside the kidney, the outer part is the cortex and the inner part is the medulla, made of cone-shaped pyramids. Blood is filtered here and urine collects in the pelvis, then flows through the ureter to the urinary bladder.
nephron.png|Nephron|Human Body|The nephron is the working unit of the kidney. Blood is filtered in the glomerulus inside Bowman's capsule. The tubule then reabsorbs useful substances like glucose, salts and water, first in the coiled parts and the loop of Henle. The remaining fluid is urine, which leaves through the collecting duct.
eye.png|Human Eye|Human Body|Light enters through the cornea and pupil, and the lens focuses it on the retina at the back. The retina has light-sensitive cells that send signals through the optic nerve to the brain. The iris controls the size of the pupil and so the amount of light entering.
ear.png|Human Ear|Human Body|The outer ear collects sound to the eardrum (tympanic membrane). Three small bones — malleus, incus and stapes — pass the vibrations to the oval window. The cochlea changes vibrations into nerve signals, and the semicircular canals help with balance.
brain.png|Human Brain|Human Body|The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain, divided into lobes: frontal (thinking and decisions), parietal (touch), temporal (hearing) and occipital (vision). The cerebellum at the back controls balance and coordination, and the medulla controls breathing and heartbeat.
respiratory.png|Respiratory System|Human Body|Air enters through the nose, passes the larynx and trachea, and splits into two bronchi, one into each lung. Inside the lungs the tubes divide into tiny branches ending in air sacs called alveoli, where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged with the blood.
skeleton.png|Human Skeleton|Human Body|The adult human skeleton has 206 bones. It supports the body, protects organs (the skull protects the brain, ribs protect the heart and lungs), and works with muscles to produce movement. Important bones include the humerus, femur, tibia, ribs and backbone.
skin.png|Human Skin|Human Body|Skin has two main layers: the outer epidermis and the thicker dermis below it. The dermis contains sweat glands, hair follicles, blood vessels and nerve endings. Skin protects the body, controls temperature through sweating, and gives the sense of touch.
flower.png|Flower Structure|Plants|The flower is the reproductive part of a plant. The male part, the stamen, has an anther (makes pollen) and a filament. The female part, the pistil, has a stigma (receives pollen), a style and an ovary containing ovules. Petals (corolla) and sepals (calyx) together form the perianth.
leaf.png|Leaf Cross-Section|Plants|A leaf slice shows the upper and lower epidermis, palisade cells packed with chloroplasts (main site of photosynthesis), and spongy cells with air spaces. Tiny pores called stomata let carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapour out.
photosynthesis.png|Photosynthesis Equation|Plants|Photosynthesis is how green plants make their food. Carbon dioxide and water, using sunlight energy trapped by chlorophyll, produce glucose (sugar) and oxygen: 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2. It happens inside chloroplasts.
dna.jpg|DNA Double Helix|Cells and Organelles|DNA carries the instructions of life. It is made of two strands twisted into a double helix. The strands are joined by base pairs: adenine always pairs with thymine, and guanine with cytosine. Genes are sections of DNA that control our characteristics.
